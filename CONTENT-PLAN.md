# Content and E-E-A-T plan

The road from "nobody knows Nooni" to **#1 on Google for "navnemærker"**.
Technical SEO and the open issues live in `SEO.md`. This file is about
content, trust and links.

**Core product:** Dots med navn (`dots-med-navn-3stk`), the name tag on
the jacket zipper. Dots med motiv comes second and Loops (`emblem`) is
secondary. This is the owner's final call; see "Product order" in
`SEARCH-GUIDE.md`.

## Strategy in one paragraph

We can't win "navnemærker" head-on yet. Established shops have years of
links and reviews. So we win the long tail first: specific searches
where Nooni is the best answer, like "navnemærke til lynlås" or
"navnemærker til børn der ikke kan læse". Each one we win brings
traffic, links and signals. Then we point all of it at one page built
to rank for the head term: `/navnemaerker`.

## The intent problem (read this first)

Most people searching "navnemærker" want labels **in** the clothes:
iron-on (strygemærker), stickers (klistermærker) or sew-in labels.
Nooni's navnemærker are different. Dots clip onto the zipper, the hat or
the mittens, and Loops hang on the bag.

That is a real advantage for some searches and a handicap for others:

- **Strong fit:** lynlås, flyverdragt, overtøj, huer og vanter,
  skoletaske, børn der ikke kan læse (symbols), selvhjulpenhed (the
  zipper pull doubles as a grip).
- **Weak fit:** "strygemærker", "navnemærker til strømper", "navnemærker
  til sutteflasker". We should not chase these with content we can't
  back with a product.

Google ranks the page that best satisfies what most searchers want. To
take #1 on the head term, `/navnemaerker` has to be the best guide to
marking children's things in Denmark, not only a product list. Longer
term, a product that covers clothes labels (stickers or iron-on, which
`design/Deks klistermærker.svg` hints are already possible) would close
the gap completely. **Decision for Camilla:** do we add one?

### About "navnemærker der tåler maskinvask"

Good instinct: it is specific, has buying intent, and it is the kind of
search where a small shop beats the big ones. **But only if it's true
for Dots.** A Dot on a jacket zipper goes in the washing machine with
the jacket. What material are they printed in (PLA softens around 60°C,
PETG holds up better)? At what temperature do they survive?

**Status (2026-10-14): Dots are wash tested and can stay on the zipper
in the machine.** The site and the drafts now say so, without naming a
temperature. Still missing for the post: the temperatures and number of
washes, and before/after photos. That test **is** the blog post, and it
is the best E-E-A-T content we can make: first-hand experience nobody
else has. Only name temperatures that were actually tested.

## E-E-A-T

Google's quality raters judge Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness
and Trust. For a small shop that mostly means: show the real person,
prove the product works, and get other sites to vouch for us.

### Experience
- Real photos of Dots on real jackets in a real garderobe, not only
  product shots. Camilla's own kids (faces optional).
- Durability and wash tests with before/after photos.
- Short videos: how to mount a Dot on a zipper, a hat, a mitten.

### Expertise
- Camilla is a trained teacher and a mum of two. That is exactly the
  expertise for selvhjulpenhed and institution topics. Say it.
- Author box on every blog post: photo, name, "lærer og mor til to",
  link to `/om-os`.
- Person schema for Camilla on `/om-os`, linked as author from every
  post.

### Authoritativeness (links and mentions)
- Local press: Dagbladet Ringkøbing-Skjern, Herning Folkeblad, TV Midtvest.
  "Mum from Borris 3D-prints name tags" is a story.
- Danish parenting bloggers and Instagram accounts: send free sets in
  exchange for an honest review (with a link).
- Gift guides: pitch to Danish gift guides before skolestart (June to
  August) and Christmas.
- Markets and fairs (Dalager Marked and others): a link from the event
  page.
- Business listings: Krak, De Gule Sider, Google Business Profile
  (Storegade 47, Borris), Trustpilot.
- Institutions: a vuggestue or børnehave that uses Dots and links to
  them on their parent info page is worth a lot.

### Trust
- Reviews: collect them from every order (Shopify review app or
  Trustpilot). Show them on product pages and add AggregateRating
  schema once we have them. Today the "Sådan virker det" strip sits
  where reviews will go.
- Already done: policies on the site, CVR, address, contact in the
  footer.
- Consider e-mærket later, once order volume justifies the fee.

## What goes on the blog

Yes, the blog matters. It is where the long tail is won. Rules:

1. **Every post answers one real question** a parent types into Google.
   The title contains the keyword.
2. **Written by Camilla, from experience.** First person, her photos.
   No generic AI filler; Google and readers both see through it.
3. **Every post links to the matching product and to `/navnemaerker`.**
   The hub collects the authority.
4. **Checklists and comparisons beat stories** for search. Stories (like
   the 3D-print post) are good for trust and Instagram, but rank less.
5. **One post a month, done well,** beats four thin ones.

## How we measure

- **Monthly:** Search Console positions for every keyword in the map,
  clicks, and which queries are new.
- **Leading signal:** long-tail keywords in the top 3. When several are
  there, the hub starts to move for the head term.
- **Goal:** #1 on "nooni" within weeks; top 3 on the long-tail list
  within 3 to 6 months; first page on "navnemærker" within a year; #1 is
  the end goal.
